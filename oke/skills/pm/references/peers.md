# PM: peer asks

Two jobs of different PMs can affect each other, and only a job's PM knows its code. So PMs ask each other (`$OKE peer ask`) and answer each other. The orchestrator gets no copy; the board shows who still owes an answer, and `$OKE_HOME/jobs/<job>/peers.md` keeps the talk.

**Asking.** `$OKE peer ask <job> "<what changes, in one or two sentences>"` prints the PMs it asked. `"asked": []`: no other PM has an open job; write `없음 (no other PM has an open job)` in 다른 경로 영향. It never asks yourself: impact on your own other jobs' paths you judge yourself. Otherwise write the job's report as `waiting` (`on: peer answers`) and end your turn: the answers come as `OKe inbox:` messages. Report the plan only when every PM it asked has answered (each answer names its PM; `$OKE_HOME/jobs/<job>/peers.md` lists the asks and answers). `$OKE peer status <job>` prints `waiting` (still owe an answer), `ruling` (never answered; the user is being asked what to do), `dropped` (that PM has no open job left, so it will not answer) and `no_answer` (they never answered and the user decided through the orchestrator's `kind: answer`): with `waiting` and `ruling` both empty, report, and name each `dropped` or `no_answer` PM in 다른 경로 영향 ("no answer: its jobs ended" or "no answer: <the user's choice>"). Your plan report (`done`) ends the talk: answers that come later are still logged in peers.md and mailed to you, but nobody waits for them; one that shows an impact is handled as under "A change other code uses shows up during work". A `question` or `blocked` report does not end it. Check it whenever you are told to continue a job that waits for answers. Work each answer into the plan; an answer that needs a reply is a new `peer ask` on the same job.

**A change other code uses shows up during work.** A worker report, a review finding or your own look shows the job now changes something other code may use (APIs, schemas, CLI flags, config keys, file formats, shared services) and the plan did not list it: give the worker a new instruction "Stop and wait: do not change anything until the next instruction." (seq + 1), then `$OKE peer ask <job> "<the change>"` and end your turn. When every asked PM answered: if the plan still holds, give the worker its next instruction; if it must change, edit plan.md and report `question` for the job with what changes and why, and wait for `kind: answer` before the worker goes on.

**Answering.** `peer ask (job <job>, round <n>): <text>` from another PM: read your open jobs' paths for what that change touches (an `Explore` subagent for a wide search, never a worker pane; call it with `run_in_background: false`: it belongs to no job of yours, and a PM that ends its turn while it owes an answer is taken for one that will not answer), then `$OKE peer answer <job> "no impact"` or `$OKE peer answer <job> "impact: <what breaks or changes here, and what you need>"`. Answer every ask, even when the answer is no impact: the asking job waits for you. A message from `oke` "Answer the peer ask of job <job>" means you have not answered yet: answer now. `peer answer` refused with "was not asked ... (or answered already)": you answered already; refused with "job ... is closed" or "unknown job": the asking job ended, so drop it. Either way, if the impact matters to your own job, report `question` for it.

**Your own job must change.** When the change hits one of your open jobs: still planning, fold it into that plan (its 다른 경로 영향 names the other job). Plan reported or later: edit its plan.md, stop its worker as under "A change other code uses shows up during work", and report `question` for your job with what changes and why; the user approves the change through the orchestrator. When one job must merge before the other, the job that merges second gets `after: <the other job>` in its plan header: `$OKE job merge` refuses it until the other one is finished (merged and verified; a folder or research job: done).

**Talking it through.** If you need something from the other PM (keep the old field one release, a different name), ask in your answer; it answers with a new `peer ask` round. When you cannot agree, report `question` for your job with both positions; the other PM does the same for its job.

**Merge order.** `$OKE job merge` refusing with `waiting for <job> to merge` (a git after-job) or `waiting for <job> to be done` (a folder or research after-job, which never merges): write the job's report as `waiting` (`on: <job>`) — not `question`: the user already approved this order — end your turn, and run the merge again when `oke` tells you "Job <job>: after-job <other> is finished" (the same mail reaches a job still waiting for the user's merge approval: there, nothing to do yet). A folder job waits for its after-job before it starts: the orchestrator starts it then. `after-job <job> was cancelled`, `was archived without a verified merge`, `is no known job` or `itself waits for <job> (a cycle)` (the refusal ends with "the orchestrator asks the user; keep the job waiting"), or the message "after-job <job> was cancelled: the orchestrator asks the user; keep the job waiting" (it reaches only a job at its merge: one still working keeps working and meets the refusal at its merge): keep the job's report `waiting` (`on: <job>`) and do not report `question` (never edit an approved plan yourself). The orchestrator asks the user, and either cancels the job or drops the after-job and tells you "after-job <job> dropped: run the merge again".
